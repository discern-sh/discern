# The homepage wall

The homepage body is one wall of small cards between the shared site header and footer. Each card states one thing discern does. A visitor scans the cards in any order and brings one group to the head of the wall with a lens. Opening a card shows a second paragraph and a link into the manual.

The wall is the site's only hydrated React island ([ADR 0430](../_adr/0430-the-homepage-ships-one-hydrated-react-island.md)). The server renders the complete page, and the browser then hydrates the wall so its controls respond. No other route sends a framework runtime to the browser.

The page is dark for every visitor, whatever their device asks for or they chose elsewhere on the site, and it offers no theme control. Its document declares [the `dark` theme](design-system-consumption.md#build-and-theme).

Each kind of movement has one element. Hover transforms belong to `.wall-card-face`. The scroll approach transforms `.wall-plane`. The drawing of a glyph that travels moves on its own box. The list item `.wall-card` changes only opacity and filter, and [the list's order alone places it](wall-columns.md). Keeping them apart stops a transform in one stylesheet from replacing a transform in another. The card test fails when `wall-card.css` moves or fades the list item, and the interactions test fails when `wall-interactions.css` moves it.

`wall.css` declares the colors of a face once, on the page's root, where the pictures of a view transition can read them. They are the foot every face ends on, a card's head, and the sheet's lighter head. The same stylesheet works out a cluster's accent from its hue for a card, the sheet, and a chip's dot.

## The headline pool

The pool is the upright first screen of the room: the eyebrow, the headline, the category line beneath it, the call to action, and a line of facts. The headline takes the pool's whole width at every size and breaks only where it must, into lines of like length. The category line follows it. Under a wrapped headline it wraps as well and is never the wider of the two. From the width at which the pool holds the headline on one line, it takes one line too.

That width is one media query in `wall.css`, which also deepens the fade down a one-line headline. The pool test names no such width. It sweeps widths from a narrow phone to a wide laptop at two heights, for a finger and for a mouse. At each it requires the headline to take the fewest lines the pool allows, even lines wherever words wrap, and the category line to follow the headline as above. It also fails when anything reaches past the pool or the page scrolls sideways.
