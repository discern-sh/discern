# Understand

These pages explain the ideas behind discern through situations you'll recognize, so you can tell what a finished change has passed, what your project keeps for the next task, and which decisions stay with you. The next time your agent says a change is done, you'll know what to look at before you decide whether it lands.

- [How discern works](how-discern-works.md): who does what, from your request to a landed change.

To answer a particular question, pick a page below.

## What has your agent finished?

- [Proof](proof.md): which of your project's tests and other commands passed, on exactly which commit, and what's left for you to judge.
- [Worktrees and trunk](worktrees-and-trunk.md): where unfinished work lives, and how a reviewed change joins your shared branch.
- [Checkpoints](checkpoints.md): how your project asks your agent a review question whenever a certain change happens, and when the answer needs your decision.

## What can your project keep for next time?

- [Standards](standards.md): how a measured improvement becomes a limit that every later change has to meet.
- [Instructions, skills, and the map](instructions-skills-and-map.md): how a rule, a method, or an explanation you teach one session reaches every later one.
- [Learn from your project's history](evidence-and-improvement.md): how your agent reads discern's local record to find what keeps slowing work down, with the counts behind each finding.

## What stays under your control?

- [What stays on your machine](local-control.md): what discern runs, records, and writes on your machine, and where your coding agent and your project's own commands go beyond it.

When you're ready to act, the [guides](../20-guides/README.md) walk you through each task. [Reference](../30-reference/README.md) has the exact fields, commands, and settings.
